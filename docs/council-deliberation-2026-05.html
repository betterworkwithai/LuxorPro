The Council of Five · Strategy Deliberation

Luxor Pro — Viability & Revenue Projection

A five-lens deliberation on whether a solo-founded, premium-paid-only personal finance SaaS can win in Brazil, plus realistic revenue projections at 3-, 6-, 12-, 24-, 36-, and 60-month horizons.

Prepared for: Luxor Pro founder · Stage: 0 paying customers, payment plumbing just shipped · Surface: web (luxorpro.com.br)

🏛️ Council Deliberation

The Adversary · Constructive Pessimist

You have zero paying customers and you’re already planning a WhatsApp bot and two native apps — that’s how solo founders die. The lifetime plan at R$ 350 is a self-inflicted wound: it caps LTV at ~R$ 350 forever while every other PFM in Brazil collects R$ 15–25/month indefinitely. Your “premium institutional” positioning collides with reality: Brazilians with R$ 1M+ in patrimônio are already on XP/BTG/Inter dashboards that are free, native, and integrated with their custody — you’re asking them to maintain a parallel system. PT-BR-only and no mobile app means you’re invisible on the channel where 70%+ of Brazilian fintech engagement happens. The 30-day money-back + 7-day trial stack means a sophisticated user gets ~37 days free, then refunds — your effective free tier is over a month. Trial→paid conversion on premium PFM in BR without a free tier is closer to 3–6%, not 5–15%.

The Architect · Visionary

The architecture you’ve built is actually defensible long-term — unified multi-currency wealth + cashflow + Open Finance is a position Mobills/Organizze structurally can’t reach without rebuilding (cashflow-first, investment-shallow), and the brokers can’t reach without becoming neutral (they won’t). The pirâmide patrimonial is a real wedge: gamified percentile positioning against IBGE/WID data is shareable, viral, and hard to copy without your willingness to publish brackets that anchor to wealth inequality. Your moat over 24–36 months isn’t features — it’s the data graph: every Pluggy connection + every PDF imported + every category corrected makes your AI categorizer smarter for the next user, and that compounds. WhatsApp is the right second surface (90% of BR adults daily, friction-free transaction logging is 10× web), but iOS/Android native is premature — a PWA gets you 80% of the value for 5% of the build cost until you have ≥1,000 paying users justifying app-store presence.

The Pragmatist · Simplifier

You have one job for the next 90 days: get to 100 paying customers. That’s R$ 2k MRR or R$ 20k in lifetime revenue — enough signal to know if anything works, not enough to matter financially. Everything that doesn’t directly produce a paid signup is a distraction: WhatsApp bot, native apps, “constant updating.” Kill the lifetime plan or 3× the price to R$ 997 — it’s bleeding LTV and the people buying it are exactly the people who’d otherwise pay annually for years. Build one lead magnet (the pirâmide calculator as a public, ungated tool that captures email after showing the result) and one acquisition channel (organic Twitter/X + LinkedIn personal brand of the founder, posting daily about wealth in BR with the calculator as the CTA). That’s it. Ship that, drive 10k visitors/month to it within 90 days, and the rest of the funnel will tell you what to fix.

The Researcher · Contextualist

The relevant comparable here isn’t Mobills (mass-market, ad-supported, freemium) — it’s Toshl (CH, ~$40/yr, ~1M users globally), Monarch Money (US, $99/yr, ~150k paid users in 3 years, ICP HNW couples), and Copilot Money (US, $95/yr, ~50k paid users). Monarch is the closest direct analog: founder-led, premium PFM, post-Mint exodus, hit $10M ARR in ~24 months. OpenView/a16z benchmarks 2024 on premium PFM: blended trial→paid converts at 4–8%, monthly churn is 5–7% in year one and settles at 3–4% in year two, and 60–70% of revenue comes from annual plans. Brazil-specific: the only premium consumer fintech to scale past 50k paid in <5 years was GuiaBolso (acquired by PicPay) and they did it on FREE freemium, then sold credit leads. The premium-paid-only PFM model in BR has no scaled success case yet — Luxor would be the first to prove it works, which is both the opportunity and the risk. Apple/Google ToS allows external payment links in BR (post-2023 ruling) — you can route mobile users through Stripe and avoid the 30% tax, this is a real edge.

The Jurist · Unbiased Observer

The four voices converge on three points and split on one. Convergent: (1) the next 90 days is a single-channel acquisition test, not a feature-build sprint; (2) the lifetime plan is mispriced and should be removed or repriced to R$ 997+; (3) WhatsApp before native apps. Divergent: the Architect sees defensible long-term positioning that justifies patience; the Adversary sees a 1-person team building a category that has no proven winner in Brazil and is structurally squeezed by free broker dashboards above and Mobills/Organizze below. Weighing the evidence: the Researcher’s Monarch comp is the strongest data point in favor — premium PFM can hit $10M ARR in 24 months with the right ICP and channel — but Monarch had $25M in venture funding for that ride, and Luxor has a solo founder with effectively zero burn capital. The honest read: viable but only in a narrow path where the founder personally becomes the acquisition channel (LinkedIn/Twitter content + pirâmide calculator wedge) for 12–18 months, lifetime gets killed, mobile defers to PWA, and revenue stays under R$ 50k MRR for the first year. Anything more ambitious requires capital the founder doesn’t have.

⚖️ Final Decree

Honest viability verdict

Conditional viable. Not viable as currently scoped (broad feature roadmap, solo team, no acquisition channel). Viable as a focused, founder-led, content-driven niche play targeting the top ~5% of BR wealth — and the bar to clear is “founder becomes a known voice in PT-BR personal finance content within 12 months.” If that doesn’t happen, the product dies regardless of how good the code is.

You are competing in a category with zero scaled winners in Brazil at the premium-paid-only tier. That’s both the opportunity (open lane) and the warning (everyone who tried before either died, sold to a free competitor, or went freemium). Monarch Money is your proof-of-concept comp in the US — but they had $25M in venture and you have you.

Highest-leverage thing to do RIGHT NOW

Stop building product features. For the next 90 days, you build one thing and you do one thing daily.

Build (one-time, ~2 weeks)

Public pirâmide patrimonial calculator — luxorpro.com.br/calculadora-piramide-patrimonial

Inputs: idade, patrimônio total (R$), composição (imóveis %, investimentos %, outros %).
Output: which tier (Base → Topo), exact percentile of BR adult population, comparison to peer age cohort, what the path looks like to climb one tier (e.g. “Para chegar em Independência aos 45 anos com 6% de retorno real, você precisa aportar R$ X/mês”).

This is your wedge because:

  • Self-contained, no signup required, fully indexable
  • Highly shareable (everyone wants to know their percentile)
  • SEO gold — “quanto preciso para ser top 1% no Brasil”, “pirâmide patrimonial brasileira”, “patrimônio classe alta brasil” — zero-competition keywords with intent
  • Captures email at the “show me the path” CTA, exactly when commercial intent peaks
  • Cites IBGE/WID/Credit Suisse → press/journalist bait (Valor, Exame, InfoMoney love this data packaged for retail)
  • Pirâmide is the unique brand asset — owning the calculator URL means you own the conversation

Do (daily, ~1hr/day, every day for 90 days straight)

LinkedIn + Twitter content from the founder’s personal account. Not the brand account. Yours.

One post per day. The format:

  • Mon/Wed/Fri: a specific finding from BR wealth data (“Apenas 4% dos brasileiros têm mais de R$ 1M em patrimônio. Aqui está como eles dividem entre imóveis, ações e renda fixa: [chart]”) — link to calculator
  • Tue/Thu: a tactical post about premium personal finance (asset allocation, IR otimização, offshore para BR investors, planejamento sucessório)
  • Weekend: founder POV / build-in-public (“Mês 2 do Luxor Pro: 23 clientes pagantes. O que aprendi com as primeiras conversas:”)

Why this works for Luxor specifically: your ICP (Independência tier and aspirants) are on LinkedIn at high density. They follow people like Thiago Nigro, Bruno Perini, Gustavo Cerbasi. There is room in PT-BR fintwit for a credible, technical, premium voice. The Pragmatist is right: there is no other channel that costs R$ 0 and reaches this ICP. Paid ads at your stage = setting money on fire because you don’t have a tuned funnel yet.

Kill / Reprice

  • Kill or reprice the R$ 350 lifetime to R$ 1,497. At R$ 350 you’re trading R$ 200/yr × 7 years of stable cashflow for one quick hit. The buyer of a R$ 350 lifetime is exactly the customer who’d happily pay R$ 200/yr indefinitely. At R$ 1,497 it becomes a real “patron tier” that signals seriousness, doesn’t cannibalize annual, and only sells to the small fraction who genuinely fear future price hikes. If repricing feels too aggressive, just kill lifetime entirely for now.
  • Cut the 30-day money-back guarantee. 7-day trial is enough. The 30-day stack hands sophisticated users a 37-day free period and is the kind of policy a fully-funded company uses to reduce signup friction. Keep “cancel anytime” prominent, drop the money-back.
  • Cut roadmap announcements. Don’t promise WhatsApp/iOS/Android publicly. Ship them when they exist. Public roadmaps create expectations you’ll fail to meet on a solo timeline.

The 90-day scorecard

If at day 90 you have:

  • ≥ 5,000 organic monthly visitors to the calculator → channel is working, scale content
  • ≥ 1,000 LinkedIn followers gained → founder brand forming, keep going
  • ≥ 30 paying customers → product-market fit signal, keep building
  • < 10 paying customers → kill or pivot. The product/channel combo is wrong. Don’t sunk-cost.

What NOT to do (explicit)

  • Do not build the WhatsApp bot before month 4
  • Do not start native app development at all this year
  • Do not buy ads
  • Do not add a free tier (yet — that’s a year-2 decision)
  • Do not redesign the dashboard again
  • Do not negotiate with Pluggy for better rates (irrelevant at this scale; need 10× more usage before that conversation matters)
  • Do not hire anyone
  • Do not write a Medium article about your tech stack

The brutal one-line summary

You built a R$ 1.5M ARR product. Now you have to become the channel that sells it. Most solo founders fail at the second part — not the first. The next 90 days decide whether this is a business or a beautifully-engineered hobby.

The product is good enough. The architecture is good enough. The pricing (after killing lifetime) is good enough. What’s missing is you, publishing, every single day, until people in your ICP can’t avoid hearing your name when wealth tracking comes up in a conversation.

That’s the job.
